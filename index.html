Engineering

What I actually do all day

Shipping a free-to-play title is maybe 30% gameplay code. The rest is tooling, data, telemetry and release plumbing — and that's the part that transfers cleanly outside games.

Gameplay systems

  • Combat, ability and status-effect systems
  • Progression, economy and difficulty curves
  • Wave directors and procedural content
  • Data-driven design via ScriptableObjects
  • Deterministic simulation for replay and testing

Tooling & pipeline

  • Custom Unity editor windows and inspectors
  • Art asset pipelines and automated import rules
  • Build automation and store submission
  • Content validation that fails loudly, early
  • Addressables and asset-size budgets

Data & live-ops

  • Game analytics instrumentation and funnels
  • A/B testing frameworks and remote config
  • REST API integration and backend contracts
  • Crash triage and live incident response
  • Retention and monetisation experiment design

About

Ewe Win Lim — Ewing

I've spent seven years building mobile games, and the part I've come to care about most isn't the gameplay code — it's everything that makes shipping repeatable. Tooling that lets designers work without waiting on me. Data pipelines that fail loudly at author time rather than quietly in production. Telemetry that answers a question the day it's asked.

At Gempak Games I hold technical direction and own the code pipeline end to end. Six titles have shipped under it, four of them inside eighteen days in late 2026 — which was only possible because of a shared framework we invested in up front rather than four codebases in parallel.

Before that, three and a half years at Lifebelt Games, where I worked on Gun Builder ELITE 2 — 100K+ installs, 4.4 stars — and built the A/B testing systems and art asset tooling the studio ran on. I started at Gamelobb in 2019 on a real-time multiplayer MOBA, which is where I learned networking the hard way.

I mentor — two intern developers so far — coordinate two overseas art studios, and use AI-assisted tooling (Claude Code, Cursor) as a daily practice rather than an experiment. Right now I'm looking for a senior role where systems and tooling are treated as first-class engineering — games or otherwise.
